That's Goldiam on one page. Below is the same story in words, built from its earnings calls and investor decks since FY24, three annual reports and the Q2 FY27 results.

Picture a window dresser who brings her own jewellery. She fills a department store's display with pieces she made and paid for, and the store pays her only for what goes out the door. The designs that keep moving get ordered outright and rolled out to every branch. The rest come back.

Goldiam International dresses the windows of large American jewellery retailers this way. It is good at it: the pieces move, the retailers keep giving it more space, and the profit on each piece is healthy. But for most of the time between making a ring and being paid for it, the ring is in someone else's window, on Goldiam's money.

Now it is opening windows of its own, in India, paid for by the first business.

Where this lives on the portal: the [Goldiam International company page](/company/GOLDIAM).

## 1. What Goldiam makes

### Jewellery for American shop windows

Goldiam designs and makes diamond-studded gold and silver jewellery in Mumbai and sells it mostly to large US retailers, department stores and wholesalers. Exports were 97.12% of the parent company's own turnover in FY26. In November 2023 management put its top five customers at roughly 50-55% of revenue, and it has given no figure since.

There are two channels. Most pieces go into stores. The rest ship straight to shoppers who order on the retailers' websites, 19.4% of export revenue in Q2 FY27.

### Lab-grown took over the tray

The stones changed in three years. Lab-grown diamonds were 23% of export revenue in Q4 FY23, 54% a year later, 82% in Q4 FY25 and 91.2% in Q2 FY27. A lab-grown piece fetched $773 in Q2 FY27 against $476 for a mined one, because it carries more diamond caratage.

Goldiam grows only about a tenth of its own stones and has no plan to add machines: it sees the margin in the jewellery, not the stone.

### A small window of its own

Since 2024 Goldiam has run ORIGEM, its own chain of lab-grown jewellery stores in India. It had 29 stores on 5 November 2026. ORIGEM's Q2 FY27 revenue was ₹11.3 Cr, about 3.9% of the group's total income. It is loss-making.

## 2. How it got here

### From loose diamonds to finished jewellery

Goldiam was incorporated in Mumbai in 1986 and began by exporting cut and polished diamonds before it moved into jewellery. In 2008 it set up Goldiam USA to market to American retailers. That subsidiary earned about 46% of FY26 group profit.

### A QIP to pay for the stores

The first ORIGEM store opened at Borivali on Dhanteras in 2024. In August 2025 Goldiam raised ₹202 Cr through a qualified institutions placement, mainly to open more of them.

### Casting in America to stay outside the tariff

The same month, the US raised its tariff on Indian jewellery to 56%. From September 2025 Goldiam's US subsidiary has cast the raw gold in America; setting and polishing stay in India, and under US Customs rulings the finished piece counts as US-made. By February 2026 the tariff had fallen to 18%, and Goldiam kept the model. Management says large rivals operate on the same rulings.

## 3. How the numbers have moved

### A dip in FY23, then three years of growth

Revenue from operations fell from ₹688 Cr in FY22 to ₹533 Cr in FY23, as higher US interest rates squeezed Goldiam's middle-market shoppers and a bridal boom faded. Lab-grown carried the recovery: ₹603 Cr in FY24, ₹781 Cr in FY25 and ₹977 Cr in FY26. Net profit went from ₹106 Cr to ₹85 Cr and then up to ₹171 Cr in FY26, helped that year by currency gains and interest on the QIP cash.

### Returns held up through the dip

Return on capital was 29% in FY22, 21% and 20% through the dip, then 25% and 26%. It stayed well above our assumed 12% cost of capital even in the year revenue fell 22%. The checks read clean: an unqualified audit and no promoter pledge.

### The profit is real, the cash is slow

This is the catch. From FY22 to FY26 Goldiam reported ₹570 Cr of net profit and ₹176 Cr of operating cash, about 31% of it. Two of the five years had operating cash outflows. Inventory went from ₹177 Cr in FY22 to ₹583 Cr in FY26, and at 30 September 2026, 58.5% of it sat with US retailers on consignment.

Nothing in the books looks wrong. This is the window-dressing model at work: a new design spends about a year and a half on a retailer's shelf before it is ordered outright, on Goldiam's money.

### Why nothing protects those returns

Our moat call is no moat. The returns came from an early move to lab-grown and from US retailers trimming vendor lists, both open to rivals of Goldiam's size. On the Q2 FY27 call management itself said large peers use the same tariff rulings and the same consignment model. ORIGEM is too young to test as a brand.

## 4. The last few quarters

### Growth sped up to 42%

Revenue from operations rose 42% in H1 FY27, to ₹600 Cr from ₹423 Cr. Total income, Goldiam's own headline, rose 50%. New US wholesalers and retailers outside the US brought over $10-12 mn of the half's sales.

### Most of the margin gain was other income

Goldiam strikes its EBITDA margin on total income, which includes currency gains, treasury interest and, in Q1 FY27, a US tariff refund. On that basis Q2 FY27 was 25.2%, up from 23.5% a year earlier. Without other income it was 20.4% against 19.9%. The business margin did rise, by half a point; most of the reported gain came from other income.

### Stock stopped piling up, dues did not

In H1 FY27 inventory eased slightly to ₹573.9 Cr while receivables rose to ₹246.5 Cr. Operating cash flow for the half was minus ₹2.7 Cr against ₹164.7 Cr of pre-tax profit. Management says the inventory build will now be curtailed.

## 5. Where growth comes from next

### More space in the same windows

The main lever is shelf space at existing customers. Goldiam's largest US customer has about $2-2.5 bn of wholesale buying a year, and Goldiam supplies $35-40 mn of it, about 2%. Retailers are dropping small vendors, and Goldiam has been gaining share.

### Its own windows in India

ORIGEM is the second lever and the main new use of capital. A store breaks even at about ₹18-19 lakh of monthly sales, and some now do. The chain as a whole lost about ₹3-3.5 Cr a quarter at the net level in Q1 and Q2 FY27. Management hopes for 45-50 stores by the end of FY27, which needs 16-21 openings in about five months.

### Our base case: 19-26% a year

Management will say only "double-digit" growth for FY27. Over the next two years, our base case has revenue growing 19-26% a year, the bull case 28-34% and the bear case 9-15%. With no margin guide, earnings grow at the same rates in each case. The number that decides which case is right is how fast US retailers keep moving their buying to Goldiam.

### Growth costs stock, not plant

Working capital, not plant. Every new design and every new store adds stock that Goldiam pays for first.

## 6. What management promised, and what it delivered

On money, the record is strong. Goldiam has met all 4 of its closed financial guides. FY25 revenue was guided to grow 15-20% or more and grew 30%. The FY25 margin band of 18-22% landed at 22.4%. The Q1 FY26 tariff dip was guided at 2-3 points and came in at 2.4. The FY26 margin, guided at no lower than 18%, was 24.3% as printed; without other income it was 20.9%, still inside the band.

On timing it is reliable but not clean: 7 slips across 7 of 18 dated threads. Every one of the 7 is an ORIGEM date. The miss that mattered was this year's store plan. In May 2026 Goldiam planned 8-10 more stores by 30 September, which would have made 32-34; it had 26 that day and 29 on 5 November. An earlier plan for 10-12 new stores in six months from November 2024 delivered five. The long-range vision has shrunk from 200-300 stores in 5-7 years to about 100 in 2-3 years.

The live guide is double-digit FY27 growth at an EBITDA margin above Q4 FY26's 23.9%. H1 already grew 42% at the reported margin.

## 7. What the price already assumes

At ₹285 on 26 November, Goldiam is worth about ₹4,288 Cr, at 18.7 times trailing earnings against a five-year median of 22.2. A reverse DCF on that price implies about 27.3% growth a year over the next couple of years. That sits just above our base case of 19-26% and just below our bull case of 28-34%. Restated from the trailing twelve months, the cases are about 17.3% and 28.3%, and the price still sits between them, near the bull case.

The P/E looks modest because it is struck on reported profit. The reverse DCF counts only half of each year's profit as cash, the floor it allows, against the 31% Goldiam has actually converted. Priced on that cash, the same price asks for growth near our bull case.

## What would make me wrong

- **The cash may be turning.** Inventory eased in H1 FY27, and management says the build will now be curtailed. Two good cash years would change this story.
- **The business margin is still rising.** Without other income, Q2 FY27 was 20.4% against 19.9%. If that keeps climbing, the reported margin is less flattered than it looks.
- **Consolidation may work like a lock.** If US retailers keep cutting vendors and Goldiam keeps winning their share, being one of the few large suppliers left could protect returns better than our moat call allows.
- **The guide is a floor.** Management said double-digit and delivered 42% in H1.

The opposite error would be to read H1's 42% as the run rate: the second half faces last year's ₹554 Cr, and new accounts take up to two years to scale.
