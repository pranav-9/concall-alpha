That's the company on one page. Below is the same story in words, built from all eight earnings calls since the December 2024 listing, ten investor decks including the August 2026 Investor Day, and both annual reports.

Picture a toll booth on the road every diamond travels, from the grower who makes it to the shop that sells it. Almost no stone gets onto the last stretch without a stamp from a lab, because the buyer wants a stranger's word on what it is. The booth charges a small fee per stone, a few percent of what the stone finally sells for, and it does not haggle.

International Gemmological Institute, IGI, runs the busiest booth on the lab-grown lane. On its own commissioned estimate it stamps 65-75% of lab-grown diamonds by value. Its traffic has grown almost every quarter. Its toll has not gone up.

That last fact is the whole story. Revenue rises because more stones pass, not because each pays more. And because the booth's main cost is the people who do the stamping, every extra car is worth more than the one before it.

## 1. What it makes

### A certificate for every stone that passes

IGI grades loose diamonds, studded jewellery and coloured gemstones for the trade and issues a report for each piece. It charges per report. In H1 FY27 it issued 7.91 Mn reports at ₹945 each on average, to more than 7,500 customers: growers, wholesalers, jewellery makers and retailers. It ran 37 laboratories and 21 schools of gemology across 10 countries in August 2026.

The company puts the fee at under 5% of the stone's retail price. That is why a buyer rarely skips it and why a grower rarely argues over it.

### Lab-grown is the busiest lane

Loose lab-grown diamonds are 56.3% of certification revenue. Natural loose stones, natural jewellery, lab-grown jewellery and coloured gemstones make up the rest. Most of the business is Indian: about a quarter of revenue came from customers outside India, 26.0% in CY2024 and 24.3% over the fifteen months to March 2026.

## 2. How it got here

### From Antwerp to the lab-grown lane

The group began in Antwerp in 1975. The Indian company, now the listed parent, was incorporated in 1999, and IGI was among the first to certify lab-grown diamonds, in 2005. Blackstone bought IGI in May 2023, and the shares listed on 20 December 2024.

### The IPO money went to the seller

The same month, the Indian company bought IGI Belgium and IGI Netherlands for ₹1,345 Cr, paid to its promoter, a Blackstone holding company, out of the IPO's proceeds. In January 2026 its US arm added AGL, a coloured-gemstone lab, for $13.2M. AGL is small, but it is why the gemstone line jumped.

### A calendar that changed mid-stride

IGI's year ran January to December until 2025. It then moved to April to March, so FY2025-26 lasted fifteen months. Every comparison below uses twelve-month periods only.

## 3. How the numbers have moved

### Profit grew faster than revenue every year

Revenue went from ₹898 Cr in CY2023 to ₹1,053 Cr in CY2024 and ₹1,229.1 Cr in CY2025. Net profit went from ₹331 Cr to ₹427 Cr and ₹531.6 Cr. The EBITDA margin climbed from 55% to 57% and then 59.9%.

The reason is the booth's cost structure. In CY2024 the Belgian lab made a loss as the war in Europe hit demand, and restructuring abroad cut costs. In CY2025 reports rose far faster than staff costs, so the margin widened even as the average fee slipped on mix.

### Returns are huge because grading needs little capital

On the company's own adjusted figures, return on equity was 47% in CY2024 and 43% in CY2025, and return on capital 48% and 45%. A grading lab needs microscopes and people, not plants. Returns eased in CY2025 only because cash piled up in deposits. Operating cash flow kept pace with profit: ₹303 Cr, ₹393 Cr and ₹501.9 Cr over the three years.

The books read clean. The one large related-party deal was that ₹1,345 Cr purchase from the promoter.

### The moat is trust, and it has not been tested

We call the moat narrow. Management describes only two labs of repute, IGI and GIA, among thousands of small ones, and some clients moved to IGI when GIA stopped full grading of lab-grown stones. But IGI has never raised its fee, and it has not lived through a real downturn as a listed company. So the moat is untested.

## 4. The last few quarters

### Traffic is back at a record

Report volume grew 27%, 21% and 26% a year in the first three quarters on the chart, fell to 11% in Q3 FY26, then rose to 16%, 17% and 26%. Q2 FY27 brought a record 4.35 Mn reports. Revenue for the quarter was ₹398 Cr, up 31%, with PAT of ₹186 Cr and an EBITDA margin of 61.4%. In H1 FY27 revenue grew 27%, of which AGL added about 3 points. The base business grew 24%.

### The toll moves only with the lane mix

The average fee per report swings with which stones come through. It fell from ₹963 to ₹854 between Q1 and Q2 FY26, and from ₹1,010 to ₹891 between the same quarters this year. Each time the cause was mix, not a price cut.

### India's own margin is slipping

The group margin is rising, but India's own margin went from 72.8% a year ago to 69.5% in Q2 FY27, on commissions and marketing. Management expects it to settle around 70%. That is the number on the poster's third panel.

## 5. Where growth comes from next

### More stones, same toll

Management's case leans on volume. Growers are adding capacity, so more lab-grown stones need grading, and IGI wants a larger share of natural stones. AGL, Italy, Botswana and Saudi Arabia are smaller add-ons. The margin is guided from 60.1% in the twelve months to March 2026 to at least 61.1% in FY27. Growth costs little: there are no plants to build.

### Our cases

Our base case has consolidated revenue growing 19-24% a year over FY27 and FY28, 18-23% without AGL, and earnings 20-25%. The bear case is 13-17% for both, and the bull case 24-28% for revenue and 25-31% for earnings. The one number that decides which is right is second-half lab-grown volume.

## 6. What management promised, and what it delivered

For calendar 2025 IGI guided revenue growth of 15-20% and delivered 17%. It pointed to EBITDA growth of 20% and delivered 23%. Its margin band of 57-64% landed at 59.9%. All three closed financial guides were met. That is too few for a verdict, so we do not score its credibility yet.

Two smaller calls from July 2025 missed. The CFO saw no dilution in the fee per report for the rest of the year, and it fell 11% the next quarter. Other expenses were to settle near ₹50 Cr a quarter, and came in at ₹56 Cr and ₹55 Cr.

For FY27 the guide was 15% revenue growth and 20% EBITDA growth, set in May 2026 and held in July. In October it was raised to growth similar to H1, with no number; management would not confirm an analyst's 25% plus or minus two.

## 7. What the price already assumes

At ₹429 the company is worth about ₹18,550 Cr. A reverse DCF on that price implies about 22.8% earnings growth a year over the next couple of years. That sits inside our base case of 20-25%, above the bear case of 13-17% and below the bull case of 25-31%.

The P/E of 27.9 sits close to its median of 28.8 since listing. Against the whole industry it is about 1.65 times the median, which says more about how unusual a high-margin grading lab is than about this stock's own history.

## What would make me wrong

- More big retailers grade their own stones. India's largest jeweller already grades its new lab-grown line in-house.
- GIA returns to full grading of lab-grown diamonds and wins back the clients who moved.
- Lab-grown stone prices keep falling until growers stop expanding, and the traffic stalls.
- India's margin keeps sliding below 70% and drags the group margin with it.
- A first real downturn shows the trust is thinner than the market share suggests.

The opposite error: volume keeps growing near 26%, the margin rises faster than guided, and the base case proves too modest.
