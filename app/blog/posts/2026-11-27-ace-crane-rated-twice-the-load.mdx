That's the Action Construction Equipment story on one page. Below is the same story in words, built from twelve earnings calls, fifteen investor presentations and three annual reports.

Every crane carries a plate with its rated load: the most it was built to lift. A crane working at half its rating is not broken. It is waiting for heavier work.

Action Construction Equipment (ACE) has built its factories like that crane. Management says the plants it already owns can make ₹5,500-6,000 Cr of machines a year. In FY26 they made ₹3,280 Cr, and cranes and construction equipment ran at about 55% of capacity at the end of September 2026.

The heavier work has been promised for a while, and more than once it has arrived a year later than the operator said. Last year the crane lifted less than the year before and charged more for each lift.

Where this lives on the portal: the [Action Construction Equipment company page](/company/ACE).

## 1. What it makes

### Most of India's pick-and-carry cranes

A pick-and-carry crane lifts a load and drives across a site with it. ACE calls itself the world's largest maker of them, with over 63% of India's mobile crane market and about 60% of tower cranes. It also makes backhoe loaders, rollers and motor graders, forklifts and other material-handling machines, and tractors and harvesters.

### Cranes carry the company

Cranes, construction equipment and material handling were 90.0% of FY26 revenue, ₹2,953.2 Cr. That segment earned 18.6% of its revenue before unallocated costs, up from 15.6% in FY24. Agricultural equipment was 7.6% of revenue and earned 1.0%, down from 3.6%.

### Sold through dealers, booked a month ahead

About half the machines go to rental fleets; the rest go to contractors, factories and logistics users. By end use, manufacturing and logistics take about 45%, construction and infrastructure about 35%, real estate about 13% and agriculture about 7%. Orders turn fast: ACE is usually booked about a month ahead, through 125+ dealer locations, and exports were 6.20% of FY26 turnover.

## 2. How it got here

### From one crane in 1995 to eight plants

Vijay Agarwal founded the company in 1995 to make hydraulic mobile cranes. It listed in 2006, moved into construction equipment and tractors in 2009 and has made its own engines since 2013. Today it runs eight plants, six of them at Dudhola in Palwal, with capacity for about 17,400 construction machines and 9,000 tractors a year.

### Defence, and a Japanese partner for the heavy end

In FY25 the defence ministry placed ACE's largest order so far, ₹420 Cr for 1,121 rough-terrain forklifts. In 2026 ACE formed ACE KATO, a 50:50 joint venture with Japan's KATO WORKS, and moved its truck, crawler and rough-terrain cranes into it. Those heavy lines, in a market where Chinese makers hold most of the share, were doing ₹50-60 Cr of revenue, under 2% of the total. The sale booked a ₹50 Cr one-time gain in Q2 FY27.

## 3. How the numbers have moved

### Revenue doubled in three years, then stalled

Revenue more than doubled, from ₹1,630 Cr in FY22 to ₹3,327 Cr in FY25. FY26 came in at ₹3,280 Cr, down 1.4%.

### Margins rebuilt, with help from the treasury

The operating margin, before other income, rose from 10.2% in FY23 to 15.4% in FY26, as material costs fell from 71.1% of revenue to 67.5%. Profit rose faster than revenue, from ₹105 Cr in FY22 to ₹415 Cr in FY26. About a fifth of pre-tax profit is now income on the cash the company holds, ₹110 Cr in FY26, which is why the decks print an 18.1% EBITDA margin rather than the 15.4% the machines earn.

### High returns, plenty of cash, a thin payout

Return on capital employed peaked at 42% in FY24 and was 32% in FY26. That figure counts over ₹1,200 Cr of investments as capital, so the plants themselves earn well above it. Operating cash flow has run ahead of profit, 1.15 times over five years pooled. The dividend has stayed at ₹2 a share since FY24, about 6-7% of profit, short of the 15-20% payout management floated in February 2024.

The forensic checks are mostly clean. No promoter shares are pledged, related-party dealings are 2.68% of revenue and contingent liabilities 6.11% of net worth. The auditor's opinion is unqualified but carries three repeat remarks, among them six buildings whose title deeds are not in the company's name.

### A narrow moat around one niche

We rate the moat narrow. ACE's edge is scale in a niche foreign makers do not serve: management says German and Chinese makers do not build a comparable pick-and-carry crane, and its share has held between about 62% and 64%. The edge stops at heavy cranes, and it is being tested in new-generation cranes, now over 40% of volume, where Escorts is roughly level.

## 4. The last few quarters

### Price, not units, carried FY26

New emission norms for construction equipment (CEV Stage V) took effect in January 2025 and raised machine prices; buyers bought ahead and then paused. Units in cranes, construction equipment and material handling fell 19%, from 13,360 to 10,853. Revenue per unit rose 17%, from ₹23.18 lakh to ₹27.21 lakh, so the segment's revenue fell far less than its volumes.

### Volumes are back in FY27

Revenue grew 20.6% in Q1 FY27 and 19.1% in Q2. Segment units rose about 20% in Q2, to 2,812, and the operating margin held at 15.0% even as steel rose again. Agricultural units fell 17%.

### Stock for the defence orders

Inventory rose from ₹605 Cr in March to ₹832 Cr in September, about ₹100 Cr of it for defence orders, with ₹144 Cr of short-term borrowing alongside. The defence order book is a little over ₹500 Cr, to be delivered over the next 18 months to two years.

## 5. Where growth comes from next

### Filling the plants already built

The bet is utilisation, not construction. FY27 is guided at 18-20% revenue growth, made of price, 7-8% taken this year, and volumes recovering after the emission switch. The ₹400 Cr tower-crane factory and any acquisition are still undecided; a new plant is due in January-February 2027.

### Defence and KATO are the next layers

Defence was about 3% of FY26 revenue and is guided to 5-6% in FY27. ACE KATO's upgraded heavy cranes are due early in FY28, with at least ₹150 Cr of revenue that year, against ₹50-60 Cr for the lines it took over.

### Our cases, and the test

Our base case has revenue growing 16-18% a year from FY26 to FY28 at a 15-16% operating margin, the band management guides to. The bull case is 20-23% a year and the bear case 8-11%, the last if the second half of FY27 disappoints the way FY25 and FY26 did. Steel, the main raw material, rose again in October, and price rises have lagged it before.

## 6. What management promised, and what it delivered

ACE guides in numbers, which makes it easy to grade. Of 18 financial guides that have closed, 10 were met or beaten. Dated promises slipped 24 times across 14 of 25 threads.

The miss that mattered is the big one. In February 2024 management said FY23's ₹2,200 Cr would double to ₹4,400 Cr by FY26. In May 2025 that moved to FY27, and in November 2025 it became ₹4,000-4,400 Cr by FY27. FY26 ended at ₹3,280 Cr, about a quarter below the original target. The longer goal moved the same way: "3x over five years" in May 2024 became ₹6,600 Cr by FY29 in August 2025, then ₹6,000-6,200 Cr by FY29, worst case FY30.

FY24's above-32% was beaten at 35%. FY25's 15-20% ended at 14.2%. FY26's 14-15% was cut, then cut again to flat, and ended at minus 1.4%. The KATO agreements were due within FY25; they were signed in March 2026, about a year late, and the joint venture started in July 2026, a quarter after the year it was due to start working.

Margins are the exception. Every annual margin guide was met on the company's own basis, which includes treasury income; without it, the margins of 13.8%, 15.2% and 15.4% in FY24-FY26 sat below what was guided. FY27's 18-20% came only in November 2026, after two quarters without a number, and the first half grew 20%.

## 7. What the price already assumes

At ₹1,321 on 26 November, ACE is worth about ₹15,731 Cr, at 32.6 times trailing earnings against a five-year median of 31.1, inside its usual range. Price to book, EV/EBITDA and market value to sales sit inside their usual ranges too.

A reverse DCF on that price implies about 20.8% earnings growth a year over the next couple of years, read as EBITDA growth. Restated from the trailing twelve months, our cases give about 16.3% in the base case, 24.9% in the bull case and 1.8% in the bear case, so the price sits between the base and the bull case, a little above the middle. Profit has compounded at 40% a year over five years from a low base, but at 13% over the last twelve months. Trailing earnings also include the ₹50 Cr KATO gain, so the multiple on the business alone is a little higher.

## What would make me wrong

- **The load may be arriving now.** The first half grew 20%, units are up, and management said utilisation had risen to 70-75% by the November call. If FY27 lands at 20% and FY28 holds the pace, the plants fill faster than our base case.
- **The share may matter less than the mix.** If new-generation cranes, where Escorts is level, reach half the market as management expects within one to three years, 63% of today's market is a weaker guide to tomorrow's.
- **Steel may win this round.** Price rises lagged steel before; another spike in the second half could hold the margin below the 15-16% band.
- **The treasury is a choice waiting to be made.** Over ₹1,200 Cr sits in investments earning less than the plants. An acquisition could use it well or badly.

The opposite error would be to read the misses as a business in trouble. Through the emission-norm year, ROCE stayed at 32% and the operating margin rose; what slipped was the timing of the growth, not the economics.

A crane rated for twice its load is not a problem until someone needs the second half of the rating. The price is set as if the heavier work arrives roughly on the schedule management gives now, not on the ones it gave before.
