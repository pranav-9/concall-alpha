That's the Unimech story on one page. Below is the same story in words, built from eight calls, seven investor decks, two annual reports and eleven broker notes.

Picture a hangar built for twice the fleet that flies out of it. The rent is paid on every bay, full or empty, and the owner's plan is simple: win enough new routes that its own planes fill the space.

Unimech Aerospace built that hangar with its December 2024 IPO. It makes the tools that jet-engine and airframe makers use to build and repair their products, and it tripled its machine capacity ahead of the orders. Then US tariffs arrived, and FY26 left about half the bays empty.

In April 2026 it did something faster than winning routes. It bought a second hangar, Hobel Bellows, that came with its planes already parked.

## The central takeaway

The record is mixed. Unimech has met 4 of its 8 closed financial guides, and its dates have mostly held, with 8 slips across 30 dated commitments. The miss that mattered most was FY26 revenue growth, guided at 35-40% and delivered at minus 1%.

The debate is no longer whether Unimech can make good tools at good margins. It is whether its own orders fill the plants it built, now that a business it bought is doing most of the visible growing.

## At a glance

- **What it sells:** aero-engine, airframe and repair tooling, 76% of Q1 FY27 revenue, mostly made to the customer's drawing.
- **Who buys:** three customers were 72% of FY26 revenue; the US was 76% of it and exports 89%.
- **FY26:** revenue of ₹240.5 Cr against ₹242.9 Cr in FY25, and profit after tax of ₹63 Cr.
- **The deal:** Hobel Bellows, about ₹129 Cr of FY26 revenue at an EBITDA margin above 50%, bought for up to ₹450 Cr.
- **Q1 FY27:** revenue of about ₹108 Cr, up 71%, and up 36% without Hobel.
- **The plants:** about 58% used in Q1 FY27, against a target of 85-90%.

## 1. It makes the tools that build jet engines, at a 70% gross margin, for a handful of buyers

Unimech machines the fixtures, stands and gauges that engine makers and their licensed suppliers need to assemble, test and overhaul engines such as the LEAP. It works mostly to the customer's drawing and sells almost entirely abroad.

The work pays well. Gross margin was about 70% in FY26, and operating margin ran at 37-38% from FY23 to FY25. The buyers are few: three customers took 72% of FY26 revenue, and the largest alone took 44.5%.

## 2. The IPO tripled its capacity, and a tariff year left half of it idle

The hangar went up in FY25. Unimech tripled its machine capacity with IPO money and guided FY26 revenue growth at 35-40%, calling 40% the minimum.

US tariffs then made its American customers stop stocking tools and order only what they needed. In November 2025 the growth guide was withdrawn for "surpass last year's revenue", and FY26 came in at ₹240.5 Cr, 1% below FY25. Q4 FY26 was guided at ₹90-100 Cr and reported ₹81.8 Cr, which management counts as met before tariff concessions.

Utilisation was meant to reach 65-70% by March 2026; it ended FY26 near 50%. Return on capital, 75% in FY24 on a small pre-IPO base, was 11% in FY26. Not everything missed: the FY26 margin guide of 30-32% was met at 31.2%.

## 3. Nothing locks the buyers in, and the largest one cut its orders by a third

Our moat call is no moat. Management describes tooling as "always a PO-to-PO business": each order stands alone, and nothing binds the customer to the next one. The approvals that let Unimech supply, aerospace certification and first-article sign-offs, are ones a funded rival can earn. Unimech earned its own after starting in 2016.

FY26 showed what that means. The largest customer's share of revenue fell from 66.46% to 44.5% as it bought about a third less, while all the other customers together grew about 64%. A healthier spread, and proof that a buyer can step back freely.

## 4. The FY27 jump is mostly a bought business: Hobel brings 21% of revenue at over 50% margin

Hobel Bellows makes metallic bellows and exhaust assemblies for engine makers in Visakhapatnam. It was consolidated from 1 May 2026, and in two months it was 21% of Q1 FY27 revenue. Its FY26 margin was above 50%, and two engine-maker groups take about 93% of its sales.

That one deal does most of the work in FY27. Management guides an EBITDA margin of 34-35% for the year and no revenue number, taking it "one quarter at a time". Our base case has reported revenue up 79-92% in FY27, about 55 points of it Hobel. With Hobel counted in both years, the same case is 18-25% a year over two years.

The second plane, Unimech's own precision parts for semiconductor, nuclear and aerospace customers, has not taken off. It was 18% of revenue in Q1 FY26 and about 3% in Q1 FY27, against a 35% target set before the deal. The own order book was ₹180.2 Cr at 30 June, but ₹87 Cr of it is one nuclear order, about half due in FY27.

## 5. The IPO cash that propped up profit has been spent, and working capital is stretching

Part of FY26's profit never came from the hangar. Other income, mostly interest on unspent IPO money, was ₹46 Cr, 58% of pre-tax profit. That cash paid for Hobel, and management expects other income to be very moderate in FY27, so profit will grow slower than operating profit.

Cash is also tied up longer. Working capital ran at about 130 days in Q1 FY27 and is guided to 160 days plus by year end. The board has approved raising up to ₹750 Cr through a share sale, which management calls an enabling resolution, with the promoters at 79.8% and a minimum public shareholding to meet.

## 6. What the price already assumes: about 72% earnings growth, between our 59% base and 75% bull cases

At ₹1,846 on 6 October, the price implies about 72% a year of near-term earnings growth. Our cases, which carry Hobel's full year and the 34-35% margin guide, give about 59% in the base case and 75% in the bull case, with 29% in the bear. The price sits in the upper half of that range.

The trailing numbers are a poor guide for now. The last twelve months hold only two months of Hobel and most of a year of interest on cash since spent, so Q2 FY27, the first full Hobel quarter, is the first clean read.

## What would make me wrong

- **The orders may already be coming.** The own order book went from ₹93.4 Cr at Q4 FY25 to ₹180.2 Cr, and the own business grew 36% in Q1 FY27.
- **Hobel could be a real lock.** Management says 70% of its products are single-sourced. If that holds through a re-sourcing round, the moat call moves.
- **Tariffs have eased.** The US rate fell from 50% to 18% by February 2026, and the FACC supply deal, $7.5 mn over five years, is a first recurring contract.

The opposite error would be to read Q1 FY27's 71% as the run rate. Most of the gap between 71% and 36% was bought, not built.

## The one question that matters

Plant utilisation: about 58% in Q1 FY27, against management's 85-90% target for mid-2027.

## What I'll be watching

- Does Q2 FY27, the first full Hobel quarter, hold the 34-35% margin guide?
- Does Unimech's own business keep growing near Q1's 36% without Hobel?
- Does working capital stop near the 160 days guided, or keep stretching?

## Final assessment

Unimech makes high-margin tools and has kept most of its dates. It has not yet shown its own orders filling the capacity its IPO paid for, and FY27's growth will mostly be Hobel's.
