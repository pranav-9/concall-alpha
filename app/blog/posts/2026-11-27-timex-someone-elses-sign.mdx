That's Timex Group India on one page. Below is the same story in words, built from its first earnings call, twelve quarters of investor decks, four annual reports and two broker notes.

Picture a watch shop on a busy street. The shopkeeper puts the watches together at a bench in the back and pays the staff, the rent and the lights. The name over the door is not his: it belongs to his family abroad, which owns just over half the shop. Other names in the window are rented from fashion houses, and on some of them the shop pays the family a fee on every watch.

For years the shop barely covered its bills. Then the crowd came. Sales rose 48% last year while the shop's own bills grew 6-8% a year, so most of each extra rupee now drops into the till.

The shopkeeper has said how far he will let that run: about 20 paise in every rupee, with the rest spent on keeping the crowd coming. And the bench in the back is full. A second one is due by next May.

Where this lives on the portal: the [Timex Group India company page](/company/TIMEX).

## 1. What Timex Group India sells

### Watches put together in Himachal, sold in India

Timex Group India is the Indian arm of the Timex Group. Its plant at Baddi, in Himachal Pradesh, assembles watches from piece parts, about 2.5 Mn of them in Q2 FY27 alone, and India took 98.0% of FY26 revenue.

### Three kinds of name, none of them its own

Management puts about 60% of revenue on the group's own brands, Timex, Helix and TMX, about 25% on licensed brands such as GUESS and Versace, and about 15% on OEM work for other companies' labels. Since April 2024 the company has paid a group company a royalty on sales of specific licensed brands: ₹27.72 Cr in FY26.

### Online is the busiest counter

E-commerce is about 40-42% of the business, against 18-19% for most watch brands by management's count. The rest goes through 5,024 multi-brand dealers, 845 defence canteens, large-format stores and 44 exclusive stores run by franchisees.

### A street with two big shops

Below ₹10,000 a watch, management describes a two-company market: Titan and Timex are the only brand owners with Indian supply chains, while imported watches pay about 22% duty. Titan is about four times Timex in the core brand, by the MD's account.

## 2. How it got here

### A joint venture with its future rival

The Timex Group came to India in 1988 through a joint venture with Jayna Times Industries, then ran one with Titan from 1990 to 2000. The company has been listed on the Bombay Stock Exchange since 1994 and a subsidiary of Timex Group Luxury Watches B.V. since 2000.

### Lean years, paid for with preference shares

For a long stretch the shop lost money. A broker's figures (Systematix, August 2026) put return on capital below zero in four of the seven years from FY15 to FY21, and reserves stood at minus ₹71 Cr in FY22. The parent kept the company funded with preference shares, and profits have started paying them back: ₹22.9 Cr were redeemed in March 2026.

### A new fee, then a smaller parent

In FY2023-24 the company took over selected Justwatches stores and the webstore, run through franchisees, and from April 2024 it began paying the royalty. Then the parent cut its stake, from 74.93% up to March 2025 to 51.00% from December 2025.

## 3. How the numbers have moved

### Sales tripled in four years

Revenue went from ₹264 Cr in FY22 to ₹383 Cr, ₹419 Cr, ₹538 Cr and ₹799 Cr in FY26: growth of 45%, 9%, 28% and 48%.

### Each profit jump had its own cause

Profit rose from ₹3 Cr in FY22 to ₹75 Cr in FY26. FY23's ₹47 Cr leaned on a one-time tax credit of ₹16.84 Cr, and in FY25 the new royalty, about 3.9% of sales, held the margin flat. FY26 shows the mechanism: EBITDA margin went from 9.2% to 14.5% as sales rose 48% and people and office costs rose in single digits.

### Returns look huge because the equity was wiped out

Return on capital was 83% in FY26 and return on equity 150%. The base is thin: common equity was negative or near zero until FY24, so profit is set against equity it has only just rebuilt. The audit opinion is unmodified, and dealings with the group, mostly watches bought from group companies plus the royalty, came to 25.8% of FY26 revenue.

### Why nothing protects those returns

Our moat call is no moat. The names belong to the group or are rented, and the brand has not shown it can lead on price: the rise of about 8% in April 2026 only countered dearer inputs. Share moves on this street, too. Titan's lead in Timex's band was about ten times five years ago and is about four times now, by management's account, and a rival with brands and a local plant could take share the same way.

## 4. The last two quarters

### Growth sped up to 47%, led online

Total income grew 30% in Q1 FY27 and 47% in Q2. In Q2, e-commerce went from about ₹108 Cr to ₹190 Cr, up 75%, and the Timex brand grew 67%, lifting own brands to about 60% of revenue from about 55%.

### The margin rose below the gross line

EBITDA margin rose from 13.0% to 16.3% in Q1 and from 17.6% to 18.2% in Q2, while the product margin fell, to 41.7% from 42.9% in Q2, on dearer movements, metals and dollars. The gain came from the bills: employee cost fell from 5.8% to 4.4% of income in Q2. For the half, the margin was about 17.5%.

### Suppliers paid for the festive stock

Stock reached ₹368 Cr on 30 September, from ₹197 Cr in March, and payables rose to ₹300 Cr from ₹121 Cr, so suppliers carried most of the build. Operating cash for the half still fell, to ₹39 Cr from ₹64 Cr. Cash has lagged profit before: over the five years to FY26, operating cash came to ₹112 Cr against ₹177 Cr of profit, about 63%.

## 5. Where growth comes from next

### A second bench by May 2027

The plant is the limit: Baddi runs at almost 100% of its 6 Mn a year. A second building is due by about May 2027, for about USD 1.2 Mn, with room for 12 Mn a year on a single shift. Lines go to 8 Mn in 2027 and towards 12 Mn over two to three years.

### 200 stores that franchisees pay for

The market leader gets about 40% of its business from about 1,350 exclusive stores, by the MD's account, against Timex Group India's 44. Management plans 200 or more over three years: about 40 in 2027, 80-100 in 2028 and 70-80 in 2029. Franchisees own and run them, so the company does not pay for the stores, and management says it needs no borrowing in the foreseeable future. What growth does cost is stock, as the second quarter showed.

### Our base case: 26-31% a year

Management gives no revenue guide, so every band here is ours. Our base case has total income growing 26-31% a year to FY28: 33-38% in FY27, then 20-25% as the new lines and stores come in. The bull case is 34-37% a year and the bear case 17-22%. Carried through a margin that reaches the guided 20%, the base case gives earnings growth of 48-54% a year from FY26.

## 6. What management promised, and what it delivered

This chapter is short because the record is. Timex Group India held its first earnings call on 4 November 2026; its earlier decks carried no outlook. On the call the MD declined to guide revenue three times.

The one financial guide is the EBITDA margin: close to about 20% for a full year "in a year or so", which we read as FY28, from 14.5% in FY26 and about 17.5% in H1 FY27. He also called 20% a ceiling, with anything above it going back into growth.

None of it has come due, so there is nothing to grade. No financial guide has closed, and none of the nine dated promises has slipped yet. The one thing already done is the capacity the decks spoke of, raised from 4 Mn to 6 Mn a year. Our credibility read is not assessable; the first real test is FY28's margin.

## 7. What the price already assumes

At ₹727 on 26 November, Timex Group India is worth about ₹7,339 Cr, at 69.2 times trailing earnings against a five-year median of 60.0, within its usual range. Market value against sales is not: 7.6 times, against a five-year median of 4.0, more than two standard deviations above its average.

A reverse DCF on that price implies about 56% earnings growth a year over the next couple of years, read as EBITDA growth. Restated from the trailing twelve months, where EBITDA already runs 31% above FY26, our cases give about 45% in the base case, 55% in the bull case and 20% in the bear case. The price sits at the bull end of the range between base and bull, close to the bull case itself.

## What would make me wrong

- **Reach may be a moat we are not counting.** Only two brand owners assemble in India behind about 22% duty, and Titan's lead fell from about ten times to about four. If share keeps moving one way, the no-moat call is too harsh.
- **The ceiling is a choice.** Spending above 20% on marketing and price is how the company plans to take share. If that works, volume compounds faster than our cases allow.
- **The bull pace is already here.** H1 grew about 40% and Q2 47%, above our base case's 33-38% for FY27.

The opposite error would be to read Q2's 18.2% margin and 47% growth as the run rate. Q3 is the seasonal low, 6.8% in Q3 FY26, the product margin is falling on input costs, and inventory is at its yearly peak.

A shop that does not own its sign can still earn more on every watch, as long as the crowd grows faster than the bills. The price is set as if the crowd keeps growing at the bull pace and the second bench fills as fast as the first.
