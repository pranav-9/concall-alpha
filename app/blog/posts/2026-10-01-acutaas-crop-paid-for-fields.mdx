That's the Acutaas Chemicals story on one page. Below is the same story in words, built from thirteen earnings calls, fifteen investor decks, three annual reports and twelve broker notes.

In June I named Acutaas, then still Ami Organics, as one of the five steepest score climbers in the [Neuland case study](/blog/product-mix-shift-neuland). This is the first full read of what sits under that climb.

Picture a farm that grew one ordinary crop for years. Then a buyer asked it to grow something to order, on contract, in a field set aside just for him. That crop sold for far more than anything else on the farm.

Acutaas is that farm. It makes pharma intermediates, the chemical steps before a drug's active ingredient, and its contract work for drug innovators (CDMO) is the special crop. The money from it is now being spent on planting two new fields: battery additives at Jhagadia and semiconductor chemicals, partly in a Korean joint venture.

There is one catch. Since FY24 the farm has stopped telling anyone how big the special harvest is.

## The central takeaway

The record is mixed. Acutaas has met 8 of its 12 closed financial guides, and the revenue guides of FY25 and FY26 were beaten. But 21 dates have slipped across 13 of its 30 dated plans, so the money lands while the projects run late.

The debate is no longer whether the margin can rise. It is whether the CDMO crop can grow from ₹90 Cr in FY24 to ₹1,000 Cr by FY28 while nobody outside the company can see the harvest.

## At a glance

- **FY26 delivered:** revenue ₹1,339 Cr, up 33%; EBITDA margin 35.9%, from 17.9% in FY24.
- **Q1 FY27:** revenue ₹329.7 Cr, up 59.1%; pharma intermediates up 76.5% and specialty chemicals down 10.6%; EBITDA margin 34.3%.
- **The FY27 guide:** 25% revenue growth, an EBITDA margin similar to FY26 with no number, and more than 25% a year for three years.
- **The record:** 8 of 12 closed financial guides met; 21 slips across 13 of 30 dated plans.
- **Cash:** profit turned into operating cash at 0.82x over five years, and free cash flow was negative in each of them.
- **What the price assumes:** about 44% growth a year for two years, at ₹3,304 on 29 September.

Where this lives on the portal: the [Acutaas Chemicals guidance page](/company/ACUTAAS#guidance-history).

## 1. The money guides mostly land; the project dates mostly slip

FY25 was first guided at 17-22% growth, raised four times to 35%, and came in at 40.3%. FY26 was guided at 25%, raised to about 30%, and came in at 33%. Its margin guide rose in steps to 32-35% and landed at 35.9%.

FY24 is the exception. It started at 20-25% growth, was cut twice to 15-18%, and closed at 16.3%. The margin guide of above 21% ended at 17.9%. Specialty chemicals also missed in FY26, at 8.0% against 10-15%.

The dates tell a different story. The Ankleshwar blocks first due by the end of Q2 FY25 were finished in Q1 FY26, three quarters later. The battery plant, first due by the end of FY25, opened on 19 January 2026, about ten months after that date. The pilot plant has moved from Q3 FY26 to Q2 FY27.

## 2. The margin doubled because the CDMO crop grew, not because prices rose

Pharma intermediates went from 79% of revenue in FY24 to 88% in FY26. The company credits more CDMO in the mix, and cost efficiencies, for the EBITDA margin rising from 23.0% in FY25 to 35.9% in FY26.

That special crop is narrow. Our moat review finds FY26's return on capital rests on one CDMO molecule, grown in a block set aside for one buyer.

FY27 is guided at a margin similar to FY26, with no number. The first quarter printed 34.3%, and battery chemicals, which earn less than pharma, are only starting to ship.

## 3. There is no fence, and FY24 showed it

Our moat call is no moat. The drug filings that name Acutaas as a supplier also name others, and local rivals price off export data. Asked about lock-in, management put it plainly: "A reference price is used to negotiate. That's it."

FY24 is the proof. Rivals using cheaper raw material cut prices by 10-30%. Acutaas kept its share, but return on capital fell below its cost.

What would build a fence is a dedicated block for one innovator's molecule, approved in every regulated market. That exists for the anchor product. It has not yet been shown for a second.

## 4. FY27 rests on four CDMO products and a battery plant that opened late

The first new field is four validated CDMO products, each worth ₹50-100 Cr a year at peak. They were first dated to FY26 and now bring revenue from H2 FY27, mostly in FY28.

The second is battery additives: 4,000 MT of VC and FEC capacity at Jhagadia. Commercial supply began in Q1 FY27, with full use guided within three years. The third product's capex moved from Q1 FY27 to the end of Q2 FY27.

Semiconductor chemicals made ₹16 Cr in FY26. The Korean plant's capex is running ahead of its dates, and its revenue is dated to FY28.

Cash is the strain. Working capital was guided at 95-105 days for FY26 and came in at 120. Capex has run ahead of operating cash in every year of the record.

## 5. What the price already assumes: about 44% a year, above our bull case

At ₹3,304 on 29 September, the price implies about 44% growth a year for the next two years. Our cases put revenue growth at 15-20% in the bear case, 25% in the base case and 30-40% in the bull case, with the margin held at FY26's level.

So the price sits above the top of our bull case. The trailing P/E of 70 is above the 59.4 median since listing, and the only full year that ran at such a pace was FY25, at 40.3%.

## What would make me wrong

- **The guide has been low two years running.** FY25 and FY26 were raised during the year and still beaten, and Q1 FY27 grew 59.1% against a 25% guide.
- **Management says it is "confident to beat" ₹1,000 Cr.** If the four products ramp as dated, the CDMO crop could pass the target.
- **Profit has compounded faster than revenue.** Profit grew about 49% a year from FY22 to FY26.

The opposite error would be to read the FY26 margin as permanent. It rests on one product's ramp, and FY24 showed how fast prices can reset.

## The one question that matters

CDMO revenue: ₹90 Cr in FY24, the last figure disclosed, against ₹1,000 Cr targeted for FY28. Until it is reported again, pharma intermediates revenue, ₹1,174 Cr in FY26, is the nearest proxy.

## What I'll be watching

- Do the four validated products show revenue in H2 FY27, as dated?
- Does battery supply grow quarter on quarter after the third product's capex finishes?
- Does working capital come back towards the 95-105 days guided for FY26?

## Final assessment

Acutaas has turned one contract crop into a doubled margin and put the proceeds into two new fields. The money guides have mostly landed. The dates, the cash, and the size of the crop that pays for all of it, are where the record is thin.
